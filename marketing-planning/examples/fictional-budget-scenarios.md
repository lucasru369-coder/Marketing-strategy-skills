# Fictional Example: Marketing Budget Scenarios

> All figures, organizations, markets, and performance assumptions in this example are invented for demonstrating a planning method. They are not benchmarks or investment advice.

## 1. Scenario context

A fictional business has an annual marketing budget envelope of **100 planning units**. Units are used instead of currency to keep the example independent of any real company's finances.

The planning team must protect existing customer programs while funding a controlled market-entry pilot.

## 2. Base allocation

| Workstream | Planning units | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Brand and content | 20 | 20% |
| Performance and demand capture | 25 | 25% |
| Market-entry pilot | 20 | 20% |
| Customer lifecycle | 15 | 15% |
| Research and measurement | 10 | 10% |
| Contingency | 10 | 10% |
| **Total** | **100** | **100%** |

The allocation is illustrative. A real allocation must be based on objectives, evidence, costs, and capacity.

## 3. Scenario comparison

| Workstream | Constrained | Base | Growth |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Brand and content | 16 | 20 | 23 |
| Performance and demand capture | 22 | 25 | 29 |
| Market-entry pilot | 12 | 20 | 25 |
| Customer lifecycle | 15 | 15 | 15 |
| Research and measurement | 8 | 10 | 10 |
| Contingency | 7 | 10 | 8 |
| **Total** | **80** | **100** | **110** |

## 4. Scenario logic

### Constrained case — 80 units

- Protect customer lifecycle activity.
- Keep essential measurement.
- Reduce market-entry scope to a smaller pilot.
- Focus paid investment on the most defensible demand opportunities.
- Defer lower-priority content production.

### Base case — 100 units

- Deliver the planned market-entry pilot.
- Maintain always-on demand capture.
- Fund core brand and education content.
- Preserve a contingency for timing or operational changes.

### Growth case — 110 units

- Expand the market-entry pilot only if early evidence supports it.
- Increase demand capture where marginal efficiency remains credible.
- Add production capacity only after delivery bottlenecks are confirmed.
- Keep customer lifecycle investment stable unless evidence supports a change.

## 5. Decision triggers

| Trigger | Potential response |
|---|---|
| Pilot demand quality exceeds the agreed threshold | Consider staged expansion |
| Product readiness is delayed | Re-sequence spend and protect evergreen activity |
| Acquisition costs rise beyond the agreed guardrail | Review channel mix and audience quality |
| Measurement quality is insufficient | Pause scale decisions until tracking is reliable |
| Budget is reduced | Apply the constrained scenario and document removed scope |

## 6. Important interpretation notes

- A larger budget does not guarantee proportionally larger results.
- Platform-attributed revenue does not prove incremental impact.
- Contingency should have an owner and release criteria.
- Budget movement requires the appropriate approval.
- Scenarios should be recalculated when assumptions materially change.
