# Fictional Cross-Market Planning Example

> **Illustrative example only.** The organization, markets, dates, budgets, and performance figures below are synthetic. They do not describe a real company or campaign.

## 1. Planning context

A fictional consumer technology brand, Northstar Audio, is preparing a 12-month marketing plan across three markets.

The planning team has a fixed annual budget and different levels of market maturity. The objective is to balance consistent brand building with locally relevant demand generation.

## 2. Portfolio objectives

| Objective | Planning intent | Example indicator |
|---|---|---|
| Build awareness | Increase qualified reach in priority audiences | Aided awareness |
| Improve consideration | Explain product benefits and use cases | Consideration lift |
| Support demand | Help audiences move from interest to purchase | Qualified visits and conversion |
| Improve learning | Build a repeatable market insight loop | Research and experiment completion |

Targets should be set only after a baseline, measurement method, and reporting owner are agreed.

## 3. Market roles

| Market | Strategic role | Planning emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Market A | Established | Efficient demand capture and retention |
| Market B | Growth | Awareness, education, and creator partnerships |
| Market C | Emerging | Market learning, channel tests, and distribution readiness |

These roles are planning assumptions, not claims about real markets.

## 4. Illustrative budget allocation

Assume a total annual budget of 1,000,000 planning units.

| Workstream | Share | Amount |
|---|---:|---:|
| Brand and reach | 30% | 300,000 |
| Product education | 20% | 200,000 |
| Demand generation | 30% | 300,000 |
| Local partnerships | 10% | 100,000 |
| Research and experimentation | 10% | 100,000 |
| **Total** | **100%** | **1,000,000** |

The allocation is an example for demonstrating planning logic. Actual allocation should reflect opportunity, cost, readiness, and expected impact.

## 5. Quarterly rhythm

| Quarter | Shared focus | Market adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Establish baselines and annual campaign foundations | Market C prioritizes research and channel validation |
| Q2 | Product education and consideration | Market B expands creator and community activity |
| Q3 | Peak seasonal demand preparation | Market A emphasizes efficient conversion |
| Q4 | Seasonal campaign and annual learning review | Each market uses locally relevant moments and channel mix |

## 6. Governance

- Global team: owns strategic direction, shared assets, and cross-market measurement definitions.
- Market teams: validate local relevance, channel feasibility, and execution requirements.
- Finance partner: confirms budget availability and approval gates.
- Analytics partner: confirms tracking, baselines, and reporting consistency.

## 7. Planning risks

| Risk | Early signal | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Local timing conflicts | Key dates or approvals are not confirmed | Validate market calendars before committing spend |
| Uneven market readiness | Assets or channel access are delayed | Use readiness gates and phased investment |
| Measurement inconsistency | Markets define metrics differently | Publish a shared KPI dictionary |
| Budget drift | Actual commitments exceed the approved envelope | Review forecast and committed spend monthly |

## 8. Review questions

1. Are market roles based on evidence or merely historical assumptions?
2. Does each initiative connect to an objective and a measurable outcome?
3. Are shared and local responsibilities explicit?
4. Is there a contingency for delayed approvals or changing conditions?
5. What evidence would justify reallocating budget between markets?
