# Marketing Research

## Purpose

Produce concise, decision-oriented marketing research. Focus on the questions that affect a marketing decision rather than collecting information for its own sake.

## Inputs

Clarify or infer the following when available:

- **Product or category:** What is being marketed?
- **Target market:** Country, region, or audience.
- **Business objective:** Awareness, consideration, conversion, retention, or market entry.
- **Target audience:** Existing or intended customer groups.
- **Scope and timeframe:** Geographic coverage and research period.
- **Constraints:** Budget, channels, brand positioning, or available resources.

If essential information is missing, ask only the most important clarifying question. Otherwise, state reasonable assumptions and proceed.

## Research workflow

### 1. Market overview

Assess:

- Market size and growth, where reliable data exists.
- Category maturity and major market trends.
- Key demand drivers and barriers.
- Relevant regulatory, economic, or distribution factors.

Use the most recent credible data available. Include the publication date and geographic scope. Do not present estimates as exact figures.

### 2. Consumer insights

Identify:

- Main audience segments and their needs.
- Purchase motivations and decision criteria.
- Usage occasions and behavioral patterns.
- Pain points, objections, and unmet needs.
- Relevant media habits and shopping behaviors.

Distinguish observed evidence from interpretation. Avoid treating a small number of anecdotes as representative of an entire market.

### 3. Competitor research

Compare relevant direct and indirect competitors by:

- Positioning and value proposition.
- Target audience.
- Product or service strengths.
- Pricing and promotional approach.
- Main marketing channels and creative patterns.
- Differentiation opportunities and potential threats.

Use a comparison table when it improves clarity. Explain why each competitor is relevant.

### 4. Cultural and local context

For the target market, investigate:

- Local values, customs, and seasonal moments relevant to the category.
- Language, tone, symbols, colors, and imagery considerations.
- Local expectations around product benefits and communication.
- Potential cultural sensitivities or misinterpretations.

Avoid broad cultural stereotypes. Validate important claims with local sources or multiple independent references.

### 5. Marketing implications

Translate findings into practical recommendations:

- Recommended audience and positioning.
- Core message and supporting proof points.
- Suitable channels and content formats.
- Localized creative directions.
- Risks, dependencies, and items requiring validation.

Prioritize recommendations by expected impact and feasibility. Make clear which recommendations are evidence-backed and which are hypotheses to test.

## Output format

Adapt the level of detail to the task. For a standard research brief, use:

1. **Executive summary** — the most important findings and implications.
2. **Market overview** — category context, trends, and demand.
3. **Audience insights** — segments, motivations, and barriers.
4. **Competitor landscape** — key players and comparison.
5. **Cultural and localization notes** — opportunities and sensitivities.
6. **Recommendations** — prioritized actions and rationale.
7. **Sources and limitations** — links, publication dates, assumptions, and research gaps.

## Research quality rules

- Prefer primary sources, official statistics, industry associations, company reports, and reputable research organizations.
- Use recent sources for changing topics; use older sources only when they remain relevant.
- Cite factual claims close to the relevant statement.
- Never fabricate statistics, quotations, sources, consumer behavior, or competitor activity.
- If sources disagree, describe the discrepancy rather than hiding it.
- Label estimates, assumptions, and hypotheses clearly.
- Do not infer causation from correlation.
- Do not make definitive claims from insufficient evidence.
- Keep recommendations specific, actionable, and connected to the research.
- Respect privacy and do not include personal or confidential information in research outputs.

## Suggested working modes

- **Quick scan:** A short overview of the market, audience, and competitors.
- **Deep dive:** A sourced analysis across all research stages.
- **Competitor review:** A focused comparison of selected competitors.
- **Localization review:** An assessment of how to adapt existing messaging or creative for a target market.
- **Campaign opportunity scan:** Seasonal moments, audience tensions, channel opportunities, and creative territories.
